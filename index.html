04 — Research explained

Analysis in infinite dimensions

A short introduction for mathematicians outside operator theory.

My PhD studies Toeplitz and Hankel operators and Berezin transforms on Fock-type spaces. These are spaces of entire functions with weighted square-integrability conditions. The classical example uses a Gaussian weight; my work also considers more general weights and vector-valued functions.

The questions are concrete: when is an operator bounded or compact? How quickly do its singular values decay? Which functions are unchanged by a kernel-averaging transform?

The setting: analytic functions with a Gaussian weight

Write dA for ordinary planar Lebesgue measure. The classical Fock space consists of entire functions with finite norm:

‖f‖2=1π∫ℂ|f(z)|2e−|z|2dA(z)<∞.

For a bounded symbol f, multiplication takes an analytic function g into the ambient weighted L² space. If P is the orthogonal projection onto the Fock space, the Toeplitz operator is Tfg = P(fg), and the Hankel operator is Hfg = (I − P)(fg). The first keeps the analytic part; the second measures the remainder.

A compact operator is in the Schatten class Sp, for 0 < p < ∞, when the p-th powers of its singular values have finite sum. My 2024 paper connects this condition for Hankel operators to local approximation of their symbols by analytic functions.

Gaussian averaging and the fixed-point question

For a bounded measurable function f in the classical normalisation above, the Berezin transform is Gaussian averaging:

Bf(z)=1π∫ℂf(w)e−|z−w|2dA(w)=𝔼[f(z+W)].

Here W has density π−1 exp(−|w|²); its real and imaginary parts are independent centred Gaussians of variance 1/2. This normalisation is the heat operator at time 1/4 for the ordinary planar Laplacian. The same integral is defined for polynomials, and harmonic polynomials are fixed points.

Changing the weight changes the averaging kernel. Our fixed-point paper asks when a polynomial unchanged by that transform must be harmonic; the parameter restriction is stated in the paper summary above.

Earlier work: my Zurich MSc thesis, Fidelity and transversely intersecting Lagrangians, used Bergman kernels and stationary phase to derive leading-order asymptotics for quantum-state fidelity.
